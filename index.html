Chapter IIThe fine printSix provisos
  1. Proviso 1, More work: about 20 hours of work a week rose to about 30. Agta who farmed more had about ten fewer hours of leisure a week.
  2. Proviso 2, Smaller bodies: average men fell from 175 cm to 160 cm and women from 165 cm to 152 cm by 3000 BC.
  3. Proviso 3, Crowd diseases: measles, smallpox and flu reached people through livestock and crowded settlements; a band of 30 on the move gives them nowhere to spread.
  4. Proviso 4, Three grains: of about 50,000 edible plants, 15 provide 90% of our calories and rice, wheat and maize two thirds of that.
  5. Proviso 5, Someone is king: stored grain created guards, counters and rulers; the first named person in history, Kushim, was a barley accountant.
  6. Proviso 6, No way back: world population grew from about 5 million in 10,000 BC to 8.2 billion today.

    Amendment IThe warning

    Malthus warns that people will always outgrow their food.

    0×as many people as when he wrote

    People multiply, he argued, while food only adds up, so famine will always close the gap. About a billion people were alive as he wrote. The contract has since fed eight times as many.

    1909

    Amendment IIThe loophole

    Haber and Bosch pull fertiliser out of the air. It now feeds half of us.

    0%of humanity fed by synthetic nitrogen

    For ten thousand years the harvest was rationed by the nitrogen in the soil. Fritz Haber made ammonia from air on a laboratory bench; by 1913 Carl Bosch had built it into a factory. About half the nitrogen in your body has passed through one.

    1960s

    Amendment IIIThe upgrade

    Semi-dwarf wheat multiplies the harvest. The contract scales.

    0×wheat per hectare since 1961

    Fed on fertiliser, tall wheat grew heavy and fell over. Norman Borlaug’s short-stemmed varieties stood up under the extra grain. India’s wheat harvest nearly doubled in six years. The new seeds came with terms of their own: more fertiliser, more water.

    2026
